Portfolio Narrative

The portfolio split cleanly in two this week. Three assets — Skye at Conway, Park at Peachtree Hills and Parks at Walnut — are performing well enough that the operating conversation has moved from filling units to raising fees and tapering incentives. Four assets are in genuine distress, and in each case the distress has a specific, nameable cause rather than a general market condition.

Skye Reserve is the dominant story by weight. At 982 units it is 17% of the portfolio, and at roughly 78% occupancy with 31 down or admin-classified units it accounts for most of the gap between the portfolio’s 88.1% unit-weighted occupancy and where it should be. The operating diagnosis this week was unusually direct: the problem is leadership vacancy, not demand. Brian cannot relocate to Tampa, Tina’s role is unfilled, and daily task ownership has been unclear for weeks. The response is concrete — Jeremy on site daily, Ed pulled in at roughly 25% for three to four months, Julie’s portfolio lightened for more site time, a regional manager in final interviews — and the early evidence is encouraging, with pending applications cleaned from 79 to 34 and lease execution accelerating. The timing matters because the MF1/Berkshire term sheet on a $10 million equity injection lands this week and the counterparty is arguing stabilized value while we are being measured on current performance.

Preserve at Riverwalk is a different kind of problem: not operating, but structural. A comparable sale implies roughly $80.55 million against a $98 million basis with $66.4 million of debt outstanding, after $7–8 million of contributions and approaching capital call 19 or 20. There is no operating fix for that arithmetic, and no operations call was held this week to attempt one.

Credit quality is the connective thread across the amber cohort. Chimney Hill posted bad debt near 19.7% by the 16th. Upland Townhomes holds 92.24% physical occupancy but only 80% collections, which cuts effective occupancy to about 72%. Skye Ridge is looking at 9% November retention. The Flats slipped to 87.5% collections. These are not four separate problems; they are one underwriting-and-concession-design problem showing up in four places. The encouraging development is that the response is finally standardizing — TransUnion eviction-risk scores layered into screening, second-chance approvals ended, concession burn-off tracked separately from true delinquency, payment-pattern columns added to drive non-renewal decisions. The Florida income-to-rent thresholds flagged separately in the AM team call, some as low as 2.0x rent with credit screening below 560, suggest the standard needs to be set at the portfolio level rather than negotiated asset by asset.

A second cross-cutting issue is documentation rather than performance. The Newmark lender-required repairs at Boardwalk, Hanley Place and Skye Oaks are past the original July 30 requirement with a hard September 30 deadline at Skye Oaks, and the binding constraint is not whether the work was done but whether ZMR can prove it. Invoices, payment records, certifications and before-and-after photos are scattered across email, Yardi and vendor records. Similar friction is showing up in the draw workflow, where lenders reference outdated capital budgets and a newly-required title search plus $175 wire on every Preserve draw keeps being rediscovered rather than built into the process.

Marketing was the week’s clearest win and the clearest source of transferable evidence. Portfolio-wide PPC conversions rose over 7% to more than 600 at roughly $32 per conversion, and the portfolio added 67 Google reviews. Skye Oaks quadrupled leases and nearly halved cost per lease after a website refresh and increased PMAX spend. Skye Reserve moved from 21 to 45 leases with cost per lease down over $100. Against that, The Flats produced zero Zillow applications or leases and Chimney Hill’s lead-to-prospect conversion fell 30%. The reallocation logic is already proven; the constraint is the speed of the approval cycle.

Every week the regional manager seat and Tina's backfill stay open, the counterparty's stabilized-value argument gets weaker and the interest-reserve gap ($4M versus $850K) gets harder to close in our favor. Close the regional hire and name a single accountable owner for down-unit remediation before the term sheet is countered.

2. Treat the Newmark repair packet as a hard Sept 30 deliverable, not a documentation exercise

Three assets are blocked on the same packet and the original July 30 requirement is already missed. The binding question is not whether the work was done - it is whether ZMR can prove it. Get explicit written lender guidance on the asphalt scope at Skye Oaks before committing budget, and set a Friday internal cutoff so Megan has a full week of float before the deadline.

3. Stop making decisions off the Chimney Hill and Upland delinquency reports until they reconcile

Chimney Hill shows discrepancies up to $30,000 from misincluded past-due notices, and Upland's effective occupancy of ~72% only became visible once concession burn-off was separated from true delinquency. Standardize one delinquency report format across both, with move-in date, 12-month late count, concession end date and eviction-risk score, then re-baseline.

4. Push the screening changes portfolio-wide rather than asset-by-asset

Upland ended second-chance approvals and began denying prior evictions and bankruptcies; Chimney Hill is building a payment-pattern column; Skye Ridge is fighting 9% November retention. These are the same problem. Florida income-to-rent thresholds as low as 2.0x and credit screening below 560 were flagged separately in the AM team call - set one portfolio standard and measure bad debt against it.

5. Redeploy marketing spend on the evidence already in hand

The Sky Oaks website refresh plus PMAX increase quadrupled leases and halved cost per lease. Parks at Walnut converts at $31.22. The Flats produces zero Zillow leases. Chimney Hill's lead-to-prospect conversion fell 30% on $46 conversions. The data supports a faster reallocation than the current property-by-property approval cycle allows.

6. Make the capital draw workflow deterministic

Title search plus a $175 wire is now required on every Preserve draw, lenders are referencing outdated capital budgets, and $130K-$173K reallocations are being rediscovered each cycle. Publish one draw SOP covering reallocation file, proof of payment, aging AP detail and title search, and separate capital from replacement draws in the file structure.

7. Resolve the rent write-off GL coding before the 2027 budget locks

$90,754 in write-offs through September 2026 are inconsistently coded, with ~$31,000 reducing income accounts instead of hitting expense. Budgets map to 5800-01 while ARA Yardi defaults to 6800-01. Net bad debt is correct but market rent reporting is at risk of misstatement. Decide the code now so the 2027 budget and September close are built on it.
